\chapter{Getting started}
On the first start \CM{} shows a welcome screen. A step indicator at the top
shows where you are (\emph{Terms of use}, \emph{Preparing your PC},
\emph{Models}); \emph{Next} and \emph{Back} move between the steps. From the
second step on, the screen explains what happens until you can use the app:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Read the notice about rights and tick \emph{I understand and accept these
        terms}.
  \item \emph{Preparing your PC}: the separation runtime (PyTorch and the AI
        tooling) is installed in the background, and a status card shows its
        progress. After the welcome the same status is the \textsf{RUNTIME}
        light in the display at the bottom right: it blinks amber while
        installing and turns green when the separation runtime is installed;
        then you can install models and separate songs. Below, the
        application shows your processor, memory and NVIDIA graphics card, the
        hardware tier (CPU, 4, 6, 8 or 12+~GB of video memory) and the
        recommended quality preset, as one tile each, once the engine has
        scanned your PC.
  \item \emph{Models}: the quick start described below, so that you hear
        your first stems a few minutes after setup. If the engine is not
        running yet, an illustrative preview of the \emph{Models} page is
        shown instead.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Continue} and \emph{Choose later} close the welcome; you can install
models on the \emph{Models} page (\autoref{ch:models}) at any time.

\section{Your first stems: the example song}
The last welcome step shows the model, or models, that the recommended preset
uses on your hardware: for example \emph{BS-RoFormer SW} on an NVIDIA card with
4~GB of video memory, or \emph{Hybrid Transformer Demucs (6 stems)} without a
compatible card. For each one you see its licence, a link to the licence's
source, its download size and the full notice you accept.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Read the notices and tick \emph{I have read these licence notices and
        accept them}.
  \item Press \emph{Install the recommended model}. The button stays disabled,
        with a note, until the separation runtime is installed; a green bar
        then shows the download.
  \item When the model is installed, the \emph{Try it with the example song}
        card appears. It shows the song (``Airplane Mode'' by Josh Woodward),
        its licence (CC BY 4.0), its source page, its length and size, what
        was changed (an excerpt from 0:45 to 1:13 with fades) and the folder
        the stems go to: your output folder from \emph{Settings}, or
        \texttt{library\textbackslash example\textbackslash stems} in the data
        folder if you have not chosen one.
  \item Press \emph{Try it with the example song}. \CM{} separates the clip
        into all six stems with the recommended preset and opens the
        \emph{Queue}; open the result in the mixer when it is done.
\end{enumerate}
The clip ships with the app, so trying it downloads nothing. Like every
Creative Commons song, its stems come with a \texttt{CREDITS.txt} that names
the artist, the source, the licence and the changes made. On \emph{New job},
\emph{Use the example song} adds the same clip to the inputs whenever you want
to try other settings.

\chapter{Choosing and installing models}\label{ch:models}
\CM{} does not include any separation model. Each model in the catalogue shows
the stems it produces, its download size, the video memory it needs and its
\emph{licence class}:
\begin{description}
  \item[permissive] an open licence is published (for example MIT);
  \item[disputed] the code is open but the status of the weights is unclear;
  \item[none] no licence has been published: all rights are reserved by default;
  \item[non-commercial] personal and research use only;
  \item[copyleft] for example GPL.
\end{description}
Models recommended for your hardware are marked. Press \emph{Review licence and
install} to open the licence dialog. It shows the licence, a \emph{Licence
source} link to the page where the licence (or the lack of one) can be checked,
and the date on which the project last verified it at the original source;
the full notice you accept can be expanded below. Read the notice, tick \emph{I accept this licence notice} and press
\emph{Download}. The model is downloaded from its original source; \CM{} never
hosts model files. While it downloads, a green bar with the percentage replaces
the button, so you can see that a large model is still advancing. The dialog
also shows the download size and the free space on the disk; when the model
does not fit (with 256~MB to spare), \emph{Download} stays disabled and the
dialog says so. Installing
models needs the separation runtime: until it is installed the buttons are
disabled and the page shows a notice. You are responsible for respecting each model's licence.
When the project corrects a model's licence information, the notice changes and
an installed model asks for the new notice to be accepted again.

\chapter{Separating stems and making a track without X}
\section{Creating a job}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item On \emph{New job}, drop audio files or folders on the dashed area, use
        \emph{Add files} or \emph{Add folder}, or paste a video or playlist link
        and press \emph{Add link}.
  \item Select the stems by clicking their cards: Vocals, Guitar, Drums, Bass,
        Piano, Other. A selected card is lit in the stem's colour.
  \item Choose what to export:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \emph{The selected stems}: one file per stem;
          \item \emph{The song without them}: one file with the song minus the
                selected stems, for playing or singing along. Keep \emph{Keep
                what the models missed} ticked for the most natural result;
          \item \emph{Both}: the stems and the song without them, from a single
                separation.
        \end{itemize}
  \item Choose the quality preset (\emph{Automatic} picks the best one for your
        hardware), the format (WAV 24-bit, FLAC or MP3 320~kbps) and the output
        folder. \emph{Automatic} learns from your own runs: if \emph{Balanced}
        turns out slower than 1.5 times the song length on your computer, it
        switches to \emph{Fast}. Settings that ran out of graphics memory twice
        are skipped next time, so later songs start sooner.
  \item If you added links, confirm that you own the content or are allowed to
        use it.
  \item Check the summary at the bottom (stems and mode) and press
        \emph{Start}. If a required model is missing, the page tells you
        which one to install.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The queue}
\emph{Queue} lists the jobs as cards with their stems, mode and preset. Every
song is shown by its title and artist, so you can tell which audio is which.
For a file they come from its tags, or from a name such as
\texttt{Artist - Title.wav} when it has none. For a video link they come from
the video's information (the track and artist of music videos, otherwise the
title and channel) as soon as the song starts. Until then, and for files
without either, the file name or the link is shown. Every song has a
waveform-style progress bar that fills in the colour of the first
stem, the current step (downloading, decoding, separating, exporting) and a
percentage. You can pause after the current song, resume, cancel and retry
failed songs. \emph{Open folder} opens the output folder in Explorer with the
first exported file selected (before any file exists it shows the folder
itself). Jobs that
were interrupted because the application was closed continue when it starts
again. Processing a song a second time, or asking for another stem of a song
already separated, reuses the previous work and finishes in seconds.

When a song fails, its error is shown under it together with a hint about
what to do: for example choosing the \emph{Fast} preset or allowing the CPU
fallback when the graphics card runs out of memory, installing a missing
model, or checking for a yt-dlp update when a video link stops working.
Fix the cause and press \emph{Retry}.

\section{File names}
Stems are saved as \texttt{<song> - <Stem>.<format>} and backing tracks as
\texttt{<song> - without <stems>.<format>}; the \emph{Both} mode writes both
kinds.

\chapter{The mixer}
When a song is finished, the queue shows a \emph{Mixer} link next to it. The
mixer plays the original song and every stem the models produced, in sync, so
you can hear the separation before you use it and build your own practice mix.

\section{Playing and comparing}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Press the round play button in the dock (or the space bar) to play
        and pause. The display shows the position and the length of the song.
        The song keeps playing if you go to another page; opening another
        song in the mixer replaces it.
  \item Click any waveform to jump to that point. Drag across the waveform to
        choose a region; \emph{Loop} repeats it until you switch looping off.
        With no region chosen, \emph{Loop} repeats the whole song.
  \item \textbf{A} plays the untouched song and \textbf{B} plays your mix, so
        you can compare them at the same position. The lit button shows which
        one you hear.
\end{itemize}

\section{Shaping your mix}
Every stem has its own row (\autoref{fig:mixer}), with its name, its number,
its controls, a level meter and its waveform:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{M} mutes the stem; \textbf{S} plays it solo. While any stem is
        soloed, only soloed stems are heard.
  \item The knob sets the stem's \emph{pan}: turn it left or right (drag up
        and down, or use the arrow keys) to move the stem towards one
        speaker. In the centre (\textsf{C}) both channels are untouched;
        towards one side the other channel fades out. Double-click the knob
        to centre it.
  \item The fader sets the stem's volume from silence to +6~dB; double-click
        it to return to 0~dB.
  \item The LED meter under the fader shows how loud the stem plays in your
        mix: green, then amber above $-12$~dBFS and red above $-3$~dBFS. A
        single LED holds the latest peak for a moment.
  \item \emph{Keep what the models missed} adds back whatever no stem captured
        (the residual), exactly as in a job's export. Untick it to hear only
        the stems.
  \item \emph{Reset} puts every stem back to 0~dB, centred, unmuted and not
        soloed.
\end{itemize}
Changes are smooth, without clicks, and take effect while the song plays.

\section{Exporting the mix}
\emph{Export this mix} saves the current settings (volumes, pans, mutes, solos
and the residual option, not the A/B choice) in full quality as
\texttt{<song> - custom mix.<format>} in the job's output folder, in the job's
format. An existing file is never overwritten: later exports are numbered
\texttt{(2)}, \texttt{(3)}, and so on. If the mix would clip, its level is
lowered just enough and the mixer tells you by how much.

\section{Long songs and memory}
The preview decodes the song first and each stem only when your settings need
it. Decoded audio takes memory (about 85~MB per stem for four minutes); for
long songs the preview uses a lower sample rate and says so. This only affects
what you hear in the mixer: exported files always keep full quality.

Songs processed before the mixer existed have no preview data; process them
again (this reuses the previous separation and takes seconds).

\chapter{Practice}\label{ch:practice}
When \CM{} separates a song it also listens for its tempo, its key and how far
its tuning is from A440. The display at the bottom right shows them as you
play: BPM, KEY and TUNE. The Practice panel under the mixer uses them.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/practice-dark}
  \caption{The Practice panel and the display: a synthetic demo song one
  semitone down, tuned to A440, slowed by the speed trainer and looping the
  Chorus section.}
\end{figure}

\section{Pitch and tempo}
\begin{description}
  \item[Pitch] moves the song by whole semitones, from 12 down to 12 up. Use
  $-1$ to play along in E flat tuning without retuning, or $+1$ to bring a
  song recorded in E flat to standard tuning.
  \item[Fine tune] moves it by cents. \emph{Tune to A440} sets it so that a
  recording that is slightly sharp or flat matches your tuner.
  \item[Tempo] plays from 50\,\% to 150\,\% of the original speed without
  changing the pitch.
\end{description}
Turn a knob with the mouse (drag up or down, Shift for fine steps), or focus it
with Tab and use the arrow keys, Page Up and Page Down; Delete or a
double-click resets it. Anywhere in the app, \texttt{[} and \texttt{]} change the
tempo by 5\,\% and \texttt{-} and \texttt{+} the pitch by a semitone. The display
shows the key and the tempo as you hear them.

\section{Loops, sections and click}
Drag on a waveform to loop a region. With \emph{Snap loops to bars} on, both
ends move to the nearest bar line. Under \emph{Sections}, stop where a part
begins and add a marker with its name (Verse, Chorus\dots); the loop button
next to a marker repeats that section up to the next marker. Markers are saved
with the song.

\emph{Click} adds a metronome that follows the song's beats, with an accent
on the first beat of each bar, at the level of its knob. \emph{Count-in} plays
one bar of clicks before the song starts. Tempo detection assumes 4/4; songs
in other metres keep the click but their bars may be off.

\section{Speed trainer}
The speed trainer loops the current region and raises the tempo by a step
every few passes until it reaches your target, for example from 70\,\% by
5\,\% every 2 passes up to 100\,\%. The display shows the current tempo and the
number of passes. Set the target below the start to slow down instead.

\section{Exporting at another pitch or tempo}
\emph{Export this mix} uses the pitch and tempo of the Practice panel, and the
file name says so, for example \texttt{My Song - custom mix (-1 st, 80 \%).wav}.
Mute an instrument first to get the track without it. Tick \emph{Also export
every stem and the click} to get a folder with each stem and the click at the
same pitch and tempo. The engine uses the same algorithm as the mixer, so the
file sounds like what you heard.

\section{MIDI from the stems}
The \emph{MIDI} card under the mixer turns stems into notes that you can edit
in a DAW or a notation program. Tick the stems you want and choose
\emph{Export MIDI}; a bar shows which stem is being converted (a 4-minute stem
takes a few seconds). The files are saved next to the stems and named like
them, for example \texttt{My Song - Bass.mid}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Vocals and bass give one note at a time, the melody or bass line;
  guitar, piano and other can give chords.
  \item Drums give kick, snare and closed hi-hat on the General MIDI drum
  channel (10).
  \item The files carry the song's tempo map from the beat grid, so in a DAW
  that follows the file's tempo the notes line up with the stems placed at the
  start. Without a beat grid the files use 120~BPM, and the card says so.
\end{itemize}
Transcription is automatic, so check the result: expect some extra notes an
octave above loud notes, missed fast or quiet notes, and no toms or cymbals
other than the hi-hat. Converting a stem again reuses the saved file.

\chapter{Library, folders and playlist import}
The \emph{Library} page keeps the music you work with in \emph{collections}:
\begin{description}
  \item[Folder] a folder of your songs, watched for new, changed and removed
  files (\emph{Watch a folder});
  \item[Video playlist] a playlist link that you paste (\emph{Add playlist});
  \item[Playlist file] a playlist exported from another application (M3U or
  CSV, \emph{Import playlist file}), matched against the songs in your watched
  folders. Nothing is downloaded when you import a playlist file.
\end{description}
Each collection shows its number of songs and when it was last synced;
\emph{Re-sync} looks for changes again, and the bin icon removes the collection
from the library (your audio files are never deleted). Open a collection to see
its songs, with title and artist, the match state, the file and, in
\emph{Separated}, the stems already exported for it. After a re-sync the
collection shows what changed: how many songs are new, removed, unchanged, to
check or not available.

\section{Reviewing matches}
Entries of an imported playlist are matched by artist, title and duration.
\emph{Ready} means a file was found; \emph{Check} means the best candidate is
uncertain (its score is shown); \emph{Not available} means nothing was found.
\emph{Choose file} picks the right song by hand and \emph{Not available} marks
an entry you do not have. Manual choices are kept on the next re-sync.

Tick the songs you want and press \emph{Separate selected}: they open on the
\emph{New job} page, where you choose the stems and the export as usual.
\emph{Select new} ticks only the matched songs that have no separated stems
yet, so a grown folder or playlist is processed without repeating work.

\section{Disk space}
The bottom of the page shows the space used by the library files, the cache
that can be freed, the models and the temporary files. \emph{Free space} keeps
everything the mixer needs for the songs in the queue and deletes cached
downloads and intermediate results; running a song again then takes longer.
It is available while no job runs, and your exported files are never touched.
Removing a finished job from the history lets \emph{Free space} reclaim its
files too.

\emph{Temporary files} lists the working folders that stopped or failed jobs
left behind (finished jobs clean up after themselves), with the folder path
and total size. \emph{Delete temporary files} first shows every entry that
will be deleted, with its size, number of files and date, and deletes them
only after you press \emph{Delete}; your library, saved stems and models are
never touched. It is available while no job runs. \emph{Open folder} opens the
temporary folder in Explorer.

\section{Video links}
Paste a video or playlist link instead of a file. Only the audio is downloaded.
You must confirm that you own the content or are allowed to use it.

\section{Your own videos}
If you publish your own performances, you do not need a link at all: download
the original file of each video from your channel's management studio (in
YouTube Studio: \emph{Content}, the video's \emph{Options} menu,
\emph{Download}) and add the files or their folder as usual. The original
upload usually has better audio than a stream.

\section{Command line}
The same workflows are available from the command line:
\begin{lstlisting}
cover-maker library add-folder "D:\Music\Practice"
cover-maker library add-playlist "https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=..."
cover-maker library import Playlist1.json --search "D:\Music"
cover-maker library sync 1        # report what is new, changed or removed
cover-maker library ls 1          # items and their status
cover-maker library match 12 "D:\Music\Band - Song.flac"   # fix a match
cover-maker library match 13 --unavailable
\end{lstlisting}

\chapter{Creative Commons sources}
The \emph{CC sources} page finds openly licensed music to practise with. Choose
a catalogue, type a title, artist or style and press \emph{Search}:
\begin{description}
  \item[ccMixter] stems, a cappellas and remixes shared by musicians. ccMixter
  only allows downloads from its own website: press \emph{Download on
  ccMixter}, save the file, then \emph{Add the downloaded file} so that it is
  copied into the library together with its credits.
  \item[Internet Archive] recordings with a Creative Commons or public-domain
  licence. \emph{Show tracks} lists the item's songs in the best available
  format, and \emph{Download} saves one into the library.
\end{description}
Only Creative Commons and public-domain results are shown. Each result shows
its licence (the badge opens the licence text) and a link to its source page.
Two badges call for attention:
\begin{description}
  \item[Non-commercial] the audio, and what you make from it, may not be used
  commercially.
  \item[No derivatives] the licence does not allow sharing adapted versions.
  Separating it for private practice is fine, but do not share stems or
  backing tracks made from it.
\end{description}
Downloaded and added files are stored in the \texttt{library\textbackslash cc}
folder with a small \texttt{.credits.json} file. \emph{Use in a new job} opens
them on the \emph{New job} page, and every job made from them writes a
\texttt{CREDITS.txt} next to the stems with the title, artist, source and
licence, ready to quote when you share your work.

The licence of a result is declared by whoever uploaded it. Be wary of
well-known songs, film or game music marked as free: the uploader may not own
those rights.

\chapter{Using \CM{} from your DAW}\label{ch:daw}
\CM{} can work from inside your \gls{daw}. The app must be open: the DAW tools
talk to the engine it runs, on this computer only.

\section{REAPER}
The REAPER script separates a song and inserts each stem as a new track.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item In REAPER choose \emph{Actions → Show action list → New action → Load
        ReaScript} and pick
        \texttt{daw/reaper/Cover\_Maker - Separate into stem tracks.lua} from the
        \CM{} source package (or the \texttt{daw} folder of a release).
  \item Select the audio item of the song (or no item, to type a file or a
        video link) and run the action.
  \item Check the file or link, list the stems you want separated by commas
        (vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano, other) and, for a link, answer
        \texttt{y} to confirm that you have the rights to that audio.
  \item When the job finishes, one track per stem appears below the song's
        track, lined up with the song; without a selected item they start at the
        edit cursor. The files are saved in the \texttt{Cover\_Maker stems}
        folder next to the project.
\end{enumerate}
The progress is visible in the \CM{} queue, and the models you use must be
installed there first (chapter~\ref{ch:models}); otherwise the script tells
you which ones are missing.

\section{VST3 plugin}
The plugin works in any VST3 host (Cubase, Studio One, Bitwig, Ableton Live,
FL Studio, REAPER\ldots). A plugin cannot create tracks, so it offers the stems
for you to drag onto tracks.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Unzip \texttt{Cover\_Maker-VST3-<version>-x64.zip} from the release
        page and copy \texttt{Cover\_Maker.vst3} to
        \texttt{C:\textbackslash Program Files\textbackslash Common Files\textbackslash VST3};
        rescan plugins in your DAW.
  \item Insert \emph{Cover\_Maker} as an effect on any stereo track and open it.
  \item Type or paste a file path or a video link, tick the stems, tick
        \emph{I have the rights to this audio} for a link, check the folder
        where the stems are saved and press \emph{Separate}.
  \item When it finishes, each stem has a play button (it plays through the
        track, at the \emph{Preview level} parameter), a handle that you drag
        onto a track or an empty area of the arrangement, and \emph{Show in
        folder}. \emph{Drag all stems} drags every file at once; some hosts
        then create one track per file.
\end{enumerate}

VST3 bindings it is built with are GPL; the rest of \CM{} keeps the MIT

\section{How the DAW finds \CM{}}
While the app runs, the engine writes a small file,
\texttt{\%LOCALAPPDATA\%\textbackslash Cover\_Maker\textbackslash run\textbackslash engine.json},
with its port and a key for DAW tools. Only your Windows account can read the
folder, the key changes every time the app starts and the file is deleted when
the app closes. Web pages cannot use it. To turn DAW access off, set the
environment variable \texttt{COVER\_MAKER\_DAW\_ACCESS} to \texttt{0} before
starting the app.

\chapter{About and credits}\label{ch:about}
The \emph{About} page, near the bottom of the icon rail, shows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Author}: a photo, the author's name, a short description and
  contact links (GitHub, email and website). The buttons open the system
  browser or mail program.
  \item \textbf{The app}: the logo, the descriptor \emph{the cover
  musician's studio}, what \CM{} does and a link to its source code.
  \item \textbf{Credits}: the main open-source components with their
  licences, a link to the full third-party notices, and a shortcut to the
  \emph{Models} page, where each model's licence is shown before you download
  it (chapter~\ref{ch:models}).
\end{itemize}

The texts follow the language chosen in \emph{Settings}. Everything on the page
comes from one file in the source code, \texttt{desktop/src/lib/about.json}; the
photo is \texttt{desktop/static/about/avatar.svg}. To personalise a build, edit
those two files: each text that users read has an \texttt{en} and an
\texttt{es} version.

\chapter{Command-line reference}
The engine can be used without the desktop application.

\begin{lstlisting}
cover-maker doctor                      # hardware, tier and recommended preset
cover-maker models list                 # catalogue with licence class
cover-maker models install bs-roformer-sw
cover-maker separate song.flac --stems vocals,guitar
cover-maker separate song.flac --stems vocals --mode without -o backing
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lY@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Option of \texttt{separate}} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
\texttt{--stems} & Comma-separated: vocals, guitar, drums, bass, piano, other. \\
\texttt{--mode} & \texttt{extract} (default) writes the stems; \texttt{without} writes the song without them; \texttt{both} writes both. \\
\texttt{--residual} & For \texttt{without} and \texttt{both}: \texttt{keep} (default) or \texttt{drop}. \\
\texttt{--preset} & \texttt{auto} (default), \texttt{fast}, \texttt{balanced} or \texttt{max}. \\
\texttt{--format} & \texttt{wav} (24-bit, default), \texttt{flac} or \texttt{mp3}. \\
\texttt{--allow-cpu} & Continue on the CPU if the GPU runs out of memory. \\
\texttt{--low-impact} & Use fewer CPU threads while a DAW is running. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Models are never downloaded silently: \texttt{models install} shows the licence
notice and asks for acceptance first.
